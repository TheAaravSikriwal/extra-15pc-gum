# Implementation plan: the virtual drone (CAD)

*Step 2b, written and OK'd 2026-10-17. It turns the decisions in the [spec](../specs/2026-10-13-phase-1-simulation.md#settled-in-the-cad-talk) into code, one test-first task at a time.*

## What it builds

The CAD layer of `pcs14`. It reads the catalog and `design/build.yaml`, builds every part of the picked drone as a solid in build123d, bolts them together for all eight module combinations, runs the checks (collisions, prop clearance, the parachute cone, the module rail, and balance with the pack trimmed), and puts out mass properties, files and pictures. Change a part in the catalog and the drone rebuilds itself. Every run plays in Rerun.

## Where things go

```
src/pcs14/cad/
  settings.py     design/cad.yaml as objects: materials, check limits, layout, wire routes
  parts/          builders for bought parts: boards, boxes, cylinders, the motor, the prop, the pack, the frame
  printed/        my own printed parts (legs, tray, rail and friends) and the module envelopes
  assembly.py     places every part, for all eight module combinations, plus the wire routes
  mass.py         mass, center of gravity and inertia
  checks.py       collisions, clearances, the parachute cone, rail fit, trim and balance
  existing.py     fetches existing CAD models into a local cache and compares them with mine
  export.py       STEP, the Gazebo and CFD meshes, GLB, 3MF
  views.py        stills in the project's look
  report.py       the CAD report and cad.json
src/pcs14/viz/    the CAD's Rerun timeline joins the sizing one
design/cad.yaml   the CAD's design inputs
cad/README.md     a pointer to the code, like sizing/
cad/cache/        existing CAD models, downloaded for comparison and renders (gitignored)
outputs/cad/      STEP, meshes, GLB, 3MF (gitignored)
docs/reports/cad/ the report, cad.json and the stills (committed)
```

`cad.json` lands in `docs/reports/cad/`, next to the sizing report's folder, rather than inside it, so each layer owns its own report.

**New dependencies:** build123d (Apache-2.0), which brings OCP and the OpenCascade kernel (LGPL-2.1 with an exception), and lib3mf (BSD) for 3MF files. All of them fit this GPL-3.0 repo. Each gets pinned to an exact version by the task that first needs it. Like the sizing model, the CAD runs on Windows or Linux, and CI checks it on Linux.

**Units:** meters and kilograms inside, like the sizing model. Millimeters and grams only in the files and the report.

**Every number has a source.** The catalog gets a `cad` block per part: its dimensions, mounting holes and connector spots, where each came from, which ones are estimates, and the record of any existing CAD model (link, format, terms, date checked), including "none found". Estimated dimensions get listed in the report, so the unproven list writes itself.

## What the parts research found

A research pass on 2026-10-17 went through every part in the build:

| Part | Existing CAD | Can it go in the repo? | Dimensions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emax E3 2814 motor | None (other brands' 2814s on GrabCAD) | No | Diameter, length and the 19 x 19 mm mount only |
| Gemfan 1050 prop | None | n/a | Diameter and bore from Gemfan; hub thickness and blade width from retailers |
| iFlight XL10 V6 frame | No official file; one community model of unknown terms | Unknown | Headline numbers only: 420 mm true X, 7 mm arms, three 3 mm plates, stack up to 19 mm |
| Holybro Kakute H7 + Tekko32 50A | Official STEP of the stack | No (Holybro's terms) | From the STEP and the product pages |
| RadioMaster RP2 | None | n/a | Simple drawing in the manual |
| Benewake TF-Luna | None | n/a | Full drawing in the datasheet |
| PJRC Teensy 4.1 | Community STEP, its author allows reuse with credit | With credit | Full drawing from PJRC |
| Ruko R111 Remote ID | None | n/a | One line of size |
| Matek M10Q GPS | Official STEP | No (all rights reserved) | Dimensioned photo |
| Raspberry Pi 5 | Official STEP | Yes, MIT licensed (but 77 MB, so it stays in the cache) | Full drawing |
| Pi Active Cooler, Camera Module 3 | Camera only, official | No | Full drawings |
| Heltec HaLow card and Pi HAT | None | n/a | Card: full drawing. HAT: nothing published |
| Matek BEC12S-PRO | None | n/a | Dimensioned photo |
| Fruity Chutes Harrier 40-42 | None | n/a | 40 x 154 mm tube; the screw pattern isn't published |
| Molicel P42A cell | It's a cylinder | n/a | 21.7 x 70.2 mm maximum |

**What gets estimated, and how:**
- **The frame:** built from iFlight's published numbers, with the plate and arm outlines traced from iFlight's product renders using the 420 mm wheelbase as the scale. Its volume times carbon fiber's density gets checked against its published 343 g, as a sanity check on the tracing.
- **The motor's bell and shaft split,** from its side photos scaled to the 45.5 mm length.
- **The HaLow HAT:** the standard Raspberry Pi HAT outline (65 x 56.5 mm, holes on 58 x 49 mm), since it looks HAT-sized in the photos.
- **The HaLow antenna:** an envelope of a 915 MHz half-wave dipole, about 164 mm, until a real antenna gets picked. It doesn't come with the card.
- **The Harrier's screw pattern:** an even spacing on its 40 mm base.

**Things the CAD has to solve:**
- **The Pi 5 stack:** the Active Cooler and the HaLow HAT want the same space above the Pi, so the V housing needs a stacking header. The HAT's Pi 5 support isn't confirmed (Heltec lists Pi 1 to 4), so that goes on the unproven list.
- **The ESC fit:** the Tekko32 is 48 mm long, more than a normal stack board, so it gets a fit check in the frame's body.
- **Two placement rules:** the GPS compass at least 10 cm from power wires, the ESC and the motors (Matek), and the Remote ID away from the GPS, the props and the belly (Ruko).
- **A missing part:** the camera needs a 22-pin Standard-Mini cable for the Pi 5, which goes into the catalog.
- **The lidar:** it can't read under 20 cm, so its height above the legs gets reported.

## The tasks

Each one is test-first: write the tests with hand-checked numbers, make them pass, commit and push with CI green.

| # | Task | What the tests check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **build123d in:** pinned, `pcs14.cad` skeleton, a `pcs14 cad` command, CI installing it | It imports, a 10 mm cube has a volume of 1000 mm³, and the command runs |
| 2 | **The catalog's `cad` block:** dimensions with sources, the list of estimates, the existing-CAD record, and a new `cad` source kind for weights that come from the CAD | A good block loads. A part with no existing-CAD record, or an estimate naming a field that doesn't exist, fails with a clear message |
| 3 | **Fill in the catalog:** a `cad` block for every part in the build, from the research, plus the missing parts (the camera cable, the HaLow antenna envelope, the frame's battery straps) | Every part in `build.yaml` has a `cad` block, and every existing-CAD record has its terms and the date checked |
| 4 | **Builders for bought parts:** boards (with their holes and connector spots), boxes, cylinders, the motor, the prop (a disk for the checks, a simple three-blade shape for pictures), the cells and the pack, picking the most compact cell layout that fits | Sizes and hole positions match the catalog's numbers, and the pack picks the expected layout for a made-up tray |
| 5 | **The frame:** the XL10 V6 from its published numbers and traced outlines, with the motor positions, the stack bay and the standoffs | 420 mm wheelbase, 7 mm arms, 3 mm plates, the motors where a true X puts them, and the weight from its volume within 25% of 343 g |
| 6 | **`design/cad.yaml`:** materials (density and print fill), the check limits, the layout (the module rail, the pack's slide range, the GPS mast, antenna mounts) and the wire routes | It loads, and the limits match the spec |
| 7 | **My printed parts and the module envelopes:** legs, battery tray, module rail and connector mount, GPS mast mount, lidar bracket, Remote ID mount, safety monitor carrier; boxes for the V housing, the parachute's holder around the Harrier tube, and the claws | Walls at least the printable minimum, a flat face to print on, weights from volume times density, and screw holes that line up with the parts they hold |
| 8 | **The assembly:** every part placed from the layout, all eight module combinations, and the wire routes as tubes about their likely gauge, with lengths | Every part is placed and touches what holds it, every combination builds, and every wire has a length |
| 9 | **Mass properties:** mass, center of gravity and inertia per combination, with each part's catalog weight spread over its solid and printed parts weighed from their geometry | Hand-checked on two made-up boxes, and the totals match the sizing model's mass budget for the same parts |
| 10 | **The checks:** collisions, props 10 mm from rigid parts and 20 mm from moving ones, the parachute cone, the module rail, and balance (no motor working more than 5% harder in a hover) with the pack slid to its best trim in each combination | Each check on a small made-up case with a hand-worked answer, then the real build's verdicts |
| 11 | **Existing CAD models:** a script that downloads them into the gitignored cache (only where the terms allow a personal download), and a comparison of their size and holes against mine | The comparison on made-up shapes. It skips cleanly when the cache is empty, so CI stays offline |
| 12 | **Rerun:** the drone building up part by part, then the eight combinations with the pack sliding to trim, the center of gravity and thrust markers, problems in magenta, the prop disks and the parachute cone, and the exploded view | It writes a recording without a viewer open, and every view opens on the whole run |
| 13 | **Exports and stills:** STEP, the Gazebo mesh, a watertight CFD mesh, a GLB with every part named, a 3MF per printed part, and front, side, top, three-quarter and exploded stills in the project's look | The STEP reads back with the same volume, the CFD mesh is watertight, the GLB has the part names, and the stills come out at the post sizes |
| 14 | **The report and the command:** `pcs14 cad` (with `--live`), the CAD report with every check for every combination, the mass properties, the estimates, the existing-CAD table and the stills, plus `cad.json` | The report matches a known-good copy for a made-up assembly, and `cad.json` matches the results |
| 15 | **Close the loop (step 2d):** `pcs14 cad --update-catalog` writes the printed parts' weights into the catalog (marked as from the CAD), then the sizing report reruns and the targets get rechecked | It only touches printed parts and marks their source. Then the real numbers get written up |

**Done when:** the full assembly has zero collisions in every module combination, every check passes or its failure is written up as a finding, and the printed parts' weights are back in the sizing model with the targets rechecked.
