# 2026-10-13: From "I want a drone" to an actual plan

*Laptop. Planning day. No hardware, no code, a lot of decisions.*

I started today with one sentence: I want to build a drone from scratch. I finished with a plan, a name, and a repo. Here's how that went.

## What "from scratch" means

The first real question was how far down "from scratch" goes. Buying a kit and flying it on Betaflight is a weekend. Writing your own flight controller is months of sensor fusion and control loops. I picked the second, with one condition: no custom circuit boards. I don't want to wait on a PCB in the mail. So it's off-the-shelf parts, all wired up and running flight code I wrote. Borrowing logic from open-source projects is fair game.

## What it should do

I want something fairly big and fairly fast, with a camera and GPS. Before any of that, though, it has to fly.

I spent a while on a VTOL quadplane, a drone with a wing that takes off straight up and then flies like a plane. Great flight times, but triple the firmware, bad at hovering, and it rules out the claws I want eventually. I dropped it. What I really wanted was flight time, and it turns out a normal quad gets a lot of that back with bigger, slower props and a Li-ion pack. So it's 8–10" props, aiming for 25–40 minutes.

## The fun stuff

A brainstorm turned up about thirty ideas. The ones that stuck:

- **Smart landing.** Back to where it took off, a spot I click on the map, land right here, or the nearest safe spot it found on a 3D map of the area.
- **A parachute** fired by a separate little computer (the Teensy 4.1 I had lying around), so it still works if my own firmware crashes.
- **An LLM on my laptop** that turns plain English into flight plans. It never touches the motors. Every plan gets checked by boring, deterministic code and approved by me before it flies.
- **Self-tuning, GPS-free flight, wind sensing, change detection between maps**, and some **claws** for later.

## Modular, because the claws can wait

The frame gets built to carry everything, but it flies bare first. Then I bolt on whatever the day needs: **V** (vision & nav), **P** (parachute), **C** (claws). One rule ties them together: no autonomous missions or flights above about 30 m without the parachute fitted.

## Simulate everything before buying anything

This is the big one. Before I spend money, every part choice goes through four layers:

1. a sizing model
2. a CAD mock-up
3. a Gazebo sim flown by PX4
4. eventually, my own firmware in that same sim

All eight combinations of modules have to hit their targets:

- **Bare:** 30 minutes
- **Mission setup:** 25 minutes, 80 km/h, and holding position in a 10 m/s wind
- **Fully loaded:** 20 minutes with a 250 g payload in the claws

The budget is around $1,000, but I'm deliberately not pricing anything until the sim says the design works.
