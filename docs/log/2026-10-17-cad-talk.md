# 2026-10-17: Seven questions before any CAD

*PC, Windows side. The parts are picked, so now the drone gets a body.*

Step 2 is the virtual drone: every real part modeled in CAD and bolted together, written as Python code with build123d, so swapping a part in the parts list rebuilds the whole drone. Same rule as last time, though: nothing gets built before the design questions are answered. So, seven of them, one at a time.

| Question | What I picked | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where do the shapes come from? | My own solids, built from each maker's published dimensions, with the outside, the mounting holes and the connectors exact. But before modeling any part, check whether a CAD model of it already exists | Most makers' 3D files can't legally be reposted in a public repo, and their quality is all over the place. Existing models still get used, from a local cache, to check my shapes and for hero renders |
| Where does everything sit? | Battery underneath, modules on top | The parachute fires straight up from above the center of gravity with nothing in its way, and hanging under it, the heavy pack keeps the drone upright so it lands on its legs |
| How far do my own parts go? | Real, printable designs for the brackets, legs, battery tray and module rail. Sized boxes for the vision housing, the parachute holder and the claws | The simple parts are cheap to do properly and give real weights. The others belong to later steps, and doing them now means doing them twice |
| What numbers do the checks use? | The center of gravity can't make any motor work more than 5% harder in a hover. Props stay 10 mm from anything rigid and 20 mm from anything that can swing. The chute gets a clear cone | Each limit is tied to what it actually costs, not a round number |
| How do I watch it? | Rerun again, plus still images in my post style on every run | The drone builds up part by part, steps through all eight module setups, and lights up anything too close to a prop |
| What comes out? | STEP, meshes for the flight sim and the wind tunnel, a web 3D file for the project site, print files for my parts, and a small data file with the weights | The printed parts' real weights go back into the sizing model, which reruns |
| Wires? | Rough routes now, the real harness in the electrical step | Props love eating wires, so the clearance check should see them, and the electrical step needs lengths to start from |

## The one I'm happiest about

The layout. FPV frames like the Nazgul XL10 are designed for the battery on top, and my first instinct was to leave it there. But then the parachute has to sit on top of the battery: high up, and in the way every time I swap packs. Flipping the battery underneath frees the whole top for the modules, puts the chute right over the center of gravity, and means that when the chute does open, the drone hangs the right way up. It costs a pair of landing legs.
