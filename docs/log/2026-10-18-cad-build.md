# 2026-10-18: The drone gets a body

*PC, Windows side. Steps 2b and 2c, with Rerun open the whole time.*

The plan for step 2 got its OK last night, and then I built straight through it: build123d in, every part described in the catalog, solids for every bought part, the frame, my own printed parts, the assembly, the weights, and the checks. That's tasks 1 to 10 of 15, test-first like always, with every new piece of geometry dropped into Rerun so I could watch the drone come together.

## The frame nobody drew

iFlight publishes no drawing of the Nazgul XL10 V6, just headline numbers. So I traced it. iFlight's product page has a clean top-down render, and the motors sit exactly 420 mm apart on the diagonal, which makes them a ruler. Measured that way, the motors came out 295.5 x 298.9 mm apart against iFlight's published 297 x 297, so the trace is good to about 1%. The arms are 15 mm wide, and the body plates are 36 mm wide, narrowing to 29 mm in the nose.

Then a sanity check: the traced carbon's volume times carbon fiber's density comes to 276 g, against 343 g published. The other fifth fits the screws, standoffs and printed mounts in the box.

## The checks failed everything, which was the point

The checks look for parts overlapping, anything too close to a prop disk (10 mm for solid parts, 20 mm for wires and antennas), anything in the parachute's way, the compass too close to power wires, and balance with the pack slid to its best spot. On their first run, every one of the eight module combinations failed:

- The battery tray ran into the lidar bracket.
- The frame's nose, the Remote ID mount and the camera bracket were all 9 mm from the front props.
- The compass sat 28 mm above the 5 V supply for the Pi, where Matek asks for 100.
- With the parachute fitted, the pack couldn't slide far enough back to balance.

Each one had a fix. The frame's nose was my own modeling shortcut: the trace showed it narrowing, and I'd drawn it full width. The other parts moved back from the front props. The 5 V supply moved down into the frame's bay, which makes it a Base part, and the module connector now carries its 5 V up to the modules, which is what the spec already said that connector should do. The pack's starting spot and the claws moved back to give the pack room to slide.

Second run: all eight pass. With the pack trimmed, no motor works more than 0.2% harder than the others. The tightest spots are the frame's nose at 10.6 mm from the front props, the camera cable at 20.5 mm, and the compass at 105 mm from the battery leads. Thin, but real.

## The last five tasks

Then the rest of the plan, still test-first:

- **Checking my parts against the makers' own models.** Where an official 3D model exists, it sits in a local cache (never in the repo) and gets compared with mine by size and hole spacing, whichever way it faces. The holes line up on the flight controller stack, the camera and the Pi 5. Matek's GPS model and Raspberry Pi's camera model each come out about 1 mm smaller than their own spec sheets, which is what mine follow.
- **A proper Rerun timeline.** The drone builds up part by part, then steps through every module combination with the pack sliding to its trim and both centers marked, then explodes and comes back together, on the project's black.
- **Exports.** A STEP assembly, a mesh for the flight sim, a watertight mesh for the wind tunnel, a web 3D file with every part named for the project site, a print file for every printed piece, and five stills in my post style. The wind-tunnel mesh caught one more bug: the battery tray's strap slots landed exactly on the pack's edges, so the straps would have run through the pack instead of around it.
- **`pcs14 cad`,** which does all of it in one go and writes a report.
- **Closing the loop.** The printed parts' real weights went into the catalog, and the sizing model reran on them.

| | Before the CAD | After |
|---|---|---|
| Mission | 2268 g | 2293 g |
| Mission's hover, pessimistic (target 21 min) | 21.9 min | 21.6 min |
| Max (limit 2500 g) | 2468 g | 2493 g |

Everything still passes. But the legs and the battery tray were never in the weight budget, and now that they are, there's almost no room left: three percent of hover margin on the pessimistic numbers, and 7 g before Max goes over. The next part that adds weight needs a real decision.
