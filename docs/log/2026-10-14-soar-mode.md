# 2026-10-14: Can a drone without wings save power like a kestrel?

*PC, Windows side. A thinking session, no code.*

I wanted to know if the drone could stay up while using less battery, passively, by using the air instead of fighting it. Something like a flying squirrel, but without bolting wings on.

The honest physics first: nothing stays up for free unless the air is giving it energy. Cut the motors on a wingless quad and it drops at roughly parachute speed. And the props are already the wings. A rotor is a spinning wing, and big slow props are an efficient one, which is why helicopters don't need fixed wings.

So the options are taking energy from the air, stopping somewhere high, or trading height for time. Here's how the ideas came out:

| Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Soar mode: park in rising air, hold position in a breeze, ride the wind on routes | On the shortlist (#31) |
| Perching on a branch, railing, ledge or roof, motors off | Already planned (#8), and the biggest saver by far |
| Solar on top, charging while perched | Maybe. In flight it about cancels its own weight. |
| Flying-squirrel membrane for gliding or landing | Still being discussed |
| A spiral descent on low throttle | On the shortlist (#34) |
| Recharging from the props on the way down | No. Under 0.5% of the pack from 120 m. |
| A lifting body | Left to the design hunt. Rough numbers say it's a wash. |
| Albatross-style soaring, or helium | No. Needs real wings, or way too much drag. |

## Soar mode

The one I'm keeping is soar mode. A kestrel can hang almost motionless over a hillside because wind hitting the slope gets deflected upward, and it just sits in that rising air. The drone can do a version of that. When it's waiting around (watching something, or between mapping legs), it looks for rising air and parks in it. Every 1 m/s of rising air cuts the power to hold altitude by very roughly 10–15%, and slope lift and thermals are typically 1–3 m/s.

It also prefers holding position in a breeze over sheltered calm air, because a quad sitting in moving air uses less power than one hovering in still air. And on routes, it flies upwind legs low, where the wind is weaker, and downwind legs higher.

It finds rising air by watching how much throttle it needs to hold altitude compared with what its weight says it should need. No new hardware, just software. The catch is the vortex ring state: holding still in strong rising air is the same as sinking into your own prop wash, which is how quads end up shaking and falling. So it won't sit in anything faster than about 2 m/s.

## The flying squirrel

The idea I liked most on paper was a membrane between the arms, like a flying squirrel, that glides the drone down in a spiral with the props off and spins them up at the last moment to land. It's still open, but the numbers aren't kind. There's only room for about 0.1 m² of membrane, so it would glide fast (about 18 m/s) and steep, and a spiral at that speed is around 90 m wide. The restart at the bottom is the risky part, and in a real emergency (dead motors, dead power, crashed firmware) it's exactly the part that can't happen. A spiral descent with the props on low gets a similar look without those problems, so that's what made the shortlist (#34). Coming straight down fast is what makes quads wobble, and moving forward on the way down keeps fresh air going through the props. It's software only, so it can arrive in Phase 7 without changing anything on the drone. The firmware just needs its landing sequence built so the descent part can be swapped.

## Also decided

Perching means hanging from or sitting on something that isn't a prepared landing spot. Hanging needs claws, and the claws only happen if I come up with a unique claw on an actual arm. That's a conversation for Phase 8. For now the drone just needs to fly.
